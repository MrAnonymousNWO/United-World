Research Archive / Sources & References

Sources & References

This section provides access to the sources, references, and archival materials that form the foundation of the research programme documented in this portal. The materials are organised by category to facilitate navigation and research.

International Law & Succession

The legal analysis of the World Succession Deed 1400/98 draws on established principles of succession in international law, including the Vienna Convention on the Succession of States in Respect of Treaties, the Vienna Convention on the Succession of States in Respect of State Property, Archives and Debts, and the extensive academic literature on state succession and the transfer of sovereign authority.

Military Infrastructure & NATO

The analysis of the Kreuzbergkaserne and its role in NATO infrastructure draws on historical records of NATO command structures, US military installations in Germany, telecommunications infrastructure documentation, and the extensive literature on the Cold War military-industrial complex and its post-Cold War legacy.

Juridical Theory & Singularity

The concept of Juridical Singularity draws on legal theory, philosophy of law, and the extensive literature on technological singularity and its implications for legal and political structures. Key theoretical sources include work on path dependence in legal systems, the sociology of law, and critical legal theory.

Technology & Governance

The Electric Technocracy framework draws on literature in technology governance, artificial intelligence, post-scarcity economics, and the political philosophy of direct digital democracy. Key sources include work on technological singularity, AI governance, universal basic income, and the future of work in a post-human economy.

Micronation & Sovereignty

The analysis of micronations and nation-building draws on constitutional theory, political philosophy, and the extensive literature on sovereignty, self-determination, and the nature of statehood in international law. Key sources include work on constitutional imagination, practical sovereignty discourse, and experimental governance structures.

Age of Transition & Mental Singularity

The concept of the Age of Transition and Mental Singularity draws on philosophy, cognitive science, futurism, and the literature on human transformation and post-work society. Key theoretical sources include work on Homo Nexus, the psychology of technological integration, and the future of human consciousness in a networked world.
